# Bre-X — Research Dossier

*Version 1. Excerpt check: 22 of 22 claims verified against their sources; the rest are listed under Open Questions.*

## Summary
Bre-X Minerals was a Calgary penny-stock company that perpetrated the largest mining fraud in history. Founded by David Walsh in 1989, it bought a remote jungle concession in Borneo in 1993 and began claiming an enormous gold discovery. Chief geologist Michael de Guzman and VP John Felderhof drove wildly escalating estimates that eventually reached 70 million troy ounces, sending the stock from pennies to CAD$286.50 (split-adjusted) and the market cap past CAD$6 billion. Indonesian President Suharto imposed a joint-venture deal with Freeport-McMoRan; Freeport's independent drilling in March 1997 found almost no gold. Days later, de Guzman fell from a helicopter over the Borneo jungle. By May 1997, an independent auditor confirmed the core samples had been salted with gold dust. The stock collapsed to zero. An estimated 40,000 investors lost their entire investments. Walsh fled to the Bahamas and died of a brain aneurysm in 1998. No one was ever criminally convicted.

## Timeline
- **March 1993**: Bre-X buys the Busang site in the Borneo jungle [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Bre-X bought the Busang site in March 1993.”
- **October 1995**: Bre-X announces a significant gold discovery at Busang; stock begins to soar [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “In October 1995, the company announced that significant amounts of gold had been discovered, sending its stock price soaring.”
- **May 1996**: Stock peaks at CAD$286.50/share; market cap exceeds CAD$6 billion [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Originally a penny stock, its stock price reached a peak at CAD$286.50/share (split adjusted) in May 1996, with a total market capitalization of over CAD$6 billion.”
- **1996**: Bre-X hires Suharto's son; Bob Hasan brokers a deal giving Bre-X 45% and Freeport-McMoRan the operating role [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Bre-X hired Suharto's son, Sigit Hardjojudanto. Bob Hasan, another Suharto acquaintance, negotiated a deal whereby Bre-X would have a 45% share, Freeport-McMoRan would run the mine, and Hasan would get a cut.”
- **1997**: Claimed deposit reaches 70 million troy ounces [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “finally, in 1997, it was 70,000,000 troy ounces (2,400 short tons; 2,200 t), as estimated by Kilborn Engineering, a division of SNC-Lavalin of Montreal.”
- **1997-03-10**: Freeport-McMoRan's independent drilling finds almost no gold at Busang [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “On March 10, 1997, Freeport-McMoRan received due diligence results that showed an insignificant amount of gold in the mine.”
- **March 1997**: Remains were found 400 metres from a logging road and could have been planted. [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_de_Guzman) — “The remains were found 400 metres from a logging road and could have been planted. According to journalist John McBeth, a body had gone missing from the morgue of the town from which the helicopter flew.”
- **1997-05-04**: Independent audit confirms core samples were falsified by salting with gold dust; fraud confirmed [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “on May 4, 1997, the firm reported that the crushed core samples that had been subjected to mineralogical examination by Bre-X's consultants were falsified by salting with gold dust.”
- **1998-06-04**: David Walsh dies of a brain aneurysm at age 52 [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walsh_%28mining%29) — “on June 4, 1998, Walsh died of a brain aneurysm at age 52, four days after suffering a massive stroke and being put onto life support while in a coma at Doctors Hospital.”
- **May 1999**: RCMP ends its investigation without pressing criminal charges against anyone [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “In May 1999, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) ended its investigation without pressing criminal charges against anyone.”
- **May 1999**: OSC charges Felderhof with insider trading — the only charges filed in the scandal [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “In May 1999, the Ontario Securities Commission (OSC) charged Felderhof with insider trading and spreading false information, the only charges filed by the OSC.”
- **2007-08-09**: Felderhof acquitted [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “On August 9, 2007, Felderholf, represented by Joseph Groia, received a not-guilty verdict on the charge of insider trading.”

## Story Beats
- **Rise:** David Walsh, a broke Calgary penny-stock promoter, bought an obscure jungle concession in Borneo for almost nothing and hired Filipino geologist Michael de Guzman. Samples appeared to show gold, and estimates climbed fast.
- **Peak:** By early 1997, Bre-X claimed a 70-million-troy-ounce deposit, turning a penny stock into a CAD$6-billion company and minting paper millionaires across Canada. Mining giants, pension funds, and Indonesian President Suharto all wanted a piece.
- **Cracks:** Suharto forced a deal that brought in Freeport-McMoRan as operator. Freeport's independent drilling crews hit the same rock Bre-X had drilled — and found almost no gold. On March 10, 1997, the results landed: the deposit was essentially empty.
- **Fall / comeback:** Days after Freeport's results, de Guzman fell from a helicopter over the Borneo jungle. An independent audit confirmed the samples had been salted with fine gold dust. The stock collapsed. An estimated 40,000 investors lost their entire investments.
- **Aftermath:** Walsh fled to the Bahamas and died of a brain aneurysm in June 1998. The RCMP closed its criminal investigation in May 1999 without charges. Felderhof, the only person formally charged, was acquitted in 2007. No one went to prison. Canada overhauled its mining-disclosure rules to prevent a repeat.
