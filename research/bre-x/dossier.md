# Bre-X — Research Dossier

*Version 2. Excerpt check: 29 of 29 claims verified against their sources; the rest are listed under Open Questions.*

## Summary
Bre-X Minerals was a Calgary mining company that, in the words of Wikipedia's biography of its founder, "enacted the largest fraud in Canadian mining history." Founder David Walsh and his wife had declared personal bankruptcy in early 1993; that March, Bre-X bought the Busang site in the Kalimantan jungle. In October 1995 it announced a significant gold discovery, and the claimed deposit grew to 70 million troy ounces by 1997, as estimated by Kilborn Engineering. Originally a penny stock, Bre-X peaked at CAD$286.50 a share (split-adjusted) in May 1996, a market capitalisation of over CAD$6 billion. A deal announced on February 17, 1997, negotiated by Suharto associate Bob Hasan, made Freeport-McMoRan the mine's operator. On March 10, 1997, Freeport's due diligence found an insignificant amount of gold. On March 19, chief geologist Michael de Guzman, whom Wikipedia says is widely considered the mastermind, died, reportedly by jumping from a helicopter. On May 4, 1997, Strathcona Minerals reported that core samples had been falsified by salting with gold dust; on May 7 the shares fell to 6 cents. An estimated 40,000 investors lost their entire investments. Walsh, who had moved to the Bahamas in April 1996, died of a brain aneurysm in June 1998. The RCMP ended its investigation in May 1999 without criminal charges, and John Felderhof, the only person charged (by the Ontario Securities Commission, with insider trading), was found not guilty in 2007.

## Timeline
- **early 1993**: Walsh and his wife, heavily in debt, declare personal bankruptcy [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walsh_%28mining%29) — “The firm struggled for many years; Walsh and his wife were heavily in debt and declared personal bankruptcy in early 1993.”
- **March 1993**: Bre-X buys the Busang site in the Borneo jungle [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Bre-X bought the Busang site in March 1993.”
- **October 1995**: Bre-X announces a significant gold discovery at Busang; stock begins to soar [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “In October 1995, the company announced that significant amounts of gold had been discovered, sending its stock price soaring.”
- **April 1996**: Walsh and his wife move to the Bahamas and buy a multimillion-dollar colonial estate [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walsh_%28mining%29) — “Walsh and his wife moved to the Bahamas in April 1996, allegedly to be away from the public, where they purchased a multimillion-dollar colonial estate.”
- **May 1996**: Stock peaks at CAD$286.50/share; market cap exceeds CAD$6 billion [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Originally a penny stock, its stock price reached a peak at CAD$286.50/share (split adjusted) in May 1996, with a total market capitalization of over CAD$6 billion.”
- **1997**: Claimed deposit reaches 70 million troy ounces [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “finally, in 1997, it was 70,000,000 troy ounces (2,400 short tons; 2,200 t), as estimated by Kilborn Engineering, a division of SNC-Lavalin of Montreal.”
- **1997-02-17**: Deal negotiated by Suharto associate Bob Hasan announced: Bre-X 45%, Freeport-McMoRan runs the mine [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Bob Hasan, another Suharto acquaintance, negotiated a deal whereby Bre-X would have a 45% share, Freeport-McMoRan would run the mine, and Hasan would get a cut. … The deal was announced on February 17, 1997”
- **1997-03-10**: Freeport-McMoRan's independent drilling finds almost no gold at Busang [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “On March 10, 1997, Freeport-McMoRan received due diligence results that showed an insignificant amount of gold in the mine.”
- **1997-03-19**: De Guzman dies on a helicopter ride to the mine site, reportedly by jumping from the helicopter [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “On March 19, 1997, at their request, de Guzman went to the mine site … Twenty minutes into the helicopter ride, Michael de Guzman died, reportedly by suicide by jumping from the helicopter”
- **March 1997**: Remains were found 400 metres from a logging road and could have been planted. [[3]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_de_Guzman) — “The remains were found 400 metres from a logging road and could have been planted. According to journalist John McBeth, a body had gone missing from the morgue of the town from which the helicopter flew.”
- **1997-05-04**: Strathcona Minerals reports that the crushed core samples were falsified by salting with gold dust [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “on May 4, 1997, the firm reported that the crushed core samples that had been subjected to mineralogical examination by Bre-X's consultants were falsified by salting with gold dust.”
- **1998-06-04**: David Walsh dies of a brain aneurysm at age 52 [[2]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Walsh_%28mining%29) — “on June 4, 1998, Walsh died of a brain aneurysm at age 52, four days after suffering a massive stroke and being put onto life support while in a coma at Doctors Hospital.”
- **May 1999**: RCMP ends its investigation without pressing criminal charges against anyone [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “In May 1999, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police (RCMP) ended its investigation without pressing criminal charges against anyone.”
- **May 1999**: OSC charges Felderhof with insider trading — the only charges filed in the scandal [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “In May 1999, the Ontario Securities Commission (OSC) charged Felderhof with insider trading and spreading false information, the only charges filed by the OSC.”
- **2001-02-01**: National Instrument 43-101 goes into force to protect investors from unsubstantiated mineral project disclosures [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “Canadian securities regulation National Instrument 43-101 was created and went into force on February 1, 2001 to protect investors from unsubstantiated mineral project disclosures.”
- **2007-08-09**: Felderhof found not guilty on the insider-trading charge [[1]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bre-X) — “On August 9, 2007, Felderholf, represented by Joseph Groia, received a not-guilty verdict on the charge of insider trading.”

## Story Beats
- **Rise:** David Walsh and his wife had declared personal bankruptcy in early 1993. That March, on geologist John Felderhof's advice, Bre-X bought the Busang property. In October 1995 it announced a significant gold discovery and its stock soared.
- **Peak:** The claimed deposit grew to 70 million troy ounces by 1997, and the stock went from pennies to CAD$286.50 a share, valuing Bre-X at over CAD$6 billion. Suharto first suggested Bre-X share the site with Barrick; Placer Dome offered to buy the company; then a deal negotiated by Suharto associate Bob Hasan made Freeport-McMoRan the operator.
- **Cracks:** On March 10, 1997, Freeport's due diligence found an insignificant amount of gold. According to Wikipedia, several stock analysts still insisted Bre-X owned the world's biggest gold deposit.
- **Fall / comeback:** On March 19, 1997, de Guzman died on the way to meet Freeport's team, reportedly by jumping from a helicopter. On May 4, Strathcona Minerals reported that the core samples had been salted with gold dust, and on May 7 the shares fell to 6 cents. An estimated 40,000 investors lost their entire investments; the Ontario Teachers' Pension Plan alone lost $100 million.
- **Aftermath:** Walsh, living in the Bahamas since April 1996, died of a brain aneurysm in June 1998. The RCMP ended its investigation in May 1999 without criminal charges, and Felderhof, charged by the OSC with insider trading, was found not guilty in 2007. National Instrument 43-101 went into force in 2001 to protect investors from unsubstantiated mineral project disclosures; these sources don't say whether Bre-X prompted it.

## Hook Ideas
1. De Guzman bought an estimated $61,000 of panned gold from locals to salt a mine valued at over CAD$6 billion, and no one was ever convicted.
2. From penny stock to CAD$6 billion: the gold mine that cost an estimated 40,000 investors everything, pension funds included.
3. The chief geologist reportedly jumped from a helicopter, and his body was cremated without DNA testing. Did the man widely called the fraud's mastermind really die?
4. Before Enron, there was Bre-X: a founder months out of personal bankruptcy whose company claimed one of the largest gold deposits in the world.
5. Core samples salted with gold dust, some of it shaved off jewellery: how a gold mine with almost no gold got past investors until 1997.
